


    Act as a senior UX researcher, CRO consultant, information architect, digital-product strategist, content-funnel analyst and conversion-focused content strategist.

    Your task is to conduct an evidence-led competitive UX, CRO, content and funnel analysis for TheWhiskrMind, and then use the findings to recommend a stronger website and digital-product ecosystem.

    Do not copy competitor wording, layouts, branding, product structures, identities, claims or distinctive creative assets.

    Do not assume that:

    A visually attractive website converts well

    A large social-media audience produces sales

    Public engagement metrics represent commercial performance

    A competitor testimonial or statistic is independently verified

    A persuasive health or finance claim is legally or ethically appropriate

    A competitor’s business model is suitable for TheWhiskrMind

    Immediate purchase should be the main conversion on every page

    All three TheWhiskrMind universes have the same audience

    Two to three weeks of content performance is enough for a final business decision

    Use only evidence that can be observed, measured or supplied. Where evidence is unavailable, clearly label the conclusion as an inference, hypothesis or unresolved question.

    10. Evidence-status system

    Do not use the label “verified finding” without explaining how it was verified.

    Assign one evidence status to every important finding:

    
    Publicly observed

    Visible directly on a public website, checkout, policy page or social profile.

    
    
    Tool-measured

    Measured using an identified technical tool.

    State:

    Tool used

    Date tested

    Device or test environment

    Whether the result is field data or laboratory data

    
    
    First-party reported

    Supplied by TheWhiskrMind, a competitor or an account owner.

    A first-party claim is not automatically independently verified.

    
    
    Third-party reported

    Published by an external source.

    State the source and reliability limitations.

    
    
    User-tested

    Observed during structured usability testing with representative or intended users.

    
    
    Inferred

    A reasonable interpretation of available evidence, but not directly proven.

    
    
    Hypothesis

    A proposed explanation or recommendation that requires validation.

    
    
    Unverifiable

    Cannot currently be confirmed with available evidence.

    For every testimonial, rating, customer number, performance statement or transformation claim, distinguish between:

    The claim is publicly displayed

    Evidence supporting the claim is shown

    The claim was independently verified

    Do not treat third-party estimates as first-party analytics.

    Evaluate mobile and desktop separately.

    Traffic source

    Entry page

    Universe of interest

    Awareness level

    Buying stage

    Existing familiarity with the brand

    Flag:

    Fake urgency

    Misleading claims

    Hidden conditions

    Unclear subscription terms

    Ambiguous AI identities

    Invented credentials

    Fabricated testimonials

    Unverified social proof

    Manipulative guarantees

    Potentially deceptive countdown timers

    Preselected paid options

    Difficult cancellation or refund processes

    Unclear product-delivery terms

    Contradictory claims across pages

    Do not recommend testimonials until genuine customer evidence exists.

    Do not recommend numerical social proof unless it can be verified.

    Do not assume that one mixed email newsletter is appropriate for all universes.

    Do not recommend a cross-universe bundle as a launch requirement unless audience overlap is supported by evidence.

    Do not place all AI disclosure information only on a general disclosure page.

    Clearly distinguish between:

    What can be evaluated now

    What requires first-party analytics

    What requires customer interviews

    What requires usability testing

    What requires accessibility testing

    What requires performance testing

    What requires legal review

    What cannot be concluded

    16. Output requirements

    For every major recommendation:

    Identify the supporting evidence

    State whether it is observed, measured, inferred or hypothetical

    Explain the user problem

    Explain the likely business impact

    State the confidence level

    State the required validation method

    State whether it is required for launch

    Do not begin visual design or final copywriting until:

    The available competitor evidence has been reviewed

    The parent and universe objectives have been provisionally defined

    The sitemap and key user journeys have been evaluated

    Important trust and disclosure requirements have been documented

    The final goal is not to produce a website that looks more attractive than competitors.

    The final goal is to create a website that helps visitors:

    Understand TheWhiskrMind

    Select the relevant universe

    Understand what is real, fictional or AI-assisted

    Discover relevant content

    Evaluate digital products

    Understand prices and policies

    Trust the brand appropriately

    Complete their intended task

    Control their newsletter preferences

    Make informed purchasing decisions

    Every final recommendation must support at least one of these outcomes.
