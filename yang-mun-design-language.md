# Yang Mun Website Design Language

## 1. Overview

The Yang Mun website uses a **calm editorial wellness-commerce design language**. It combines the emotional softness of a spiritual retreat, the credibility of an established teacher, and the clarity of a modern direct-to-consumer product page.

The experience is deliberately quiet rather than visually aggressive. It avoids loud gradients, glossy SaaS effects, heavy animation, sharp geometry, and crowded product grids. Instead, it relies on:

- Warm natural colors
- Large, emotionally resonant photography
- Editorial typography
- Generous whitespace
- Softly rounded containers
- Clear product pathways
- Repeated trust signals
- Simple, reassuring copy
- Subtle sales emphasis rather than hard-selling pressure

A suitable one-line description is:

> **A warm, nature-led editorial wellness style with premium book publishing cues and conversion-focused e-commerce structure.**

---

# 2. Brand Personality

The visual identity communicates the following qualities:

| Attribute | Expression in the interface |
|---|---|
| Calm | Soft contrast, whitespace, muted backgrounds, relaxed pacing |
| Wise | Editorial serif typography, book-cover styling, reflective copy |
| Human | Friendly portraits, natural expressions, real-life testimonials |
| Grounded | Earth colors, forests, wood, robes, sunlight and natural textures |
| Trustworthy | Ratings, customer counts, clear inclusions and repeated proof |
| Accessible | Plain language, simple choices, short daily commitment |
| Spiritual but practical | Meditative imagery paired with structured programs and clear outcomes |
| Premium | Carefully composed photography, restrained palette and elegant typography |

The design should feel **peaceful, mature and emotionally safe**, never clinical, futuristic, overly mystical or aggressively commercial.

---

# 3. Core Design Principles

## 3.1 Calm Before Conversion

The design first reduces tension and then introduces the offer. Commercial elements are present, but they are visually integrated into the calm experience.

Use:

- Gentle language
- Spacious sections
- Minimal competing calls to action
- Soft visual transitions
- Clear next steps

Avoid:

- Flashing urgency
- Countdown timers
- Neon sale colors
- Multiple equally dominant buttons
- Dense popups
- High-pressure scarcity patterns

## 3.2 Nature as Emotional Proof

Nature is not decorative filler. It represents balance, restoration and timeless wisdom.

Preferred settings include:

- Forest paths
- Morning sunlight
- Lakes and mountains
- Temple interiors
- Wood flooring
- Gardens
- Soft atmospheric haze
- Golden-hour landscapes

## 3.3 Editorial Authority

The brand resembles a thoughtful book publisher as much as an online store.

Key editorial cues:

- Elegant serif headlines
- Book-cover compositions
- Strong title hierarchy
- Long-form storytelling
- Chapters, weeks and journeys
- Quiet centered statements
- Refined image captions

## 3.4 Structured Transformation

Programs are framed as understandable journeys rather than vague promises.

Common structure:

1. Present the emotional problem
2. Reassure the visitor
3. Offer two clear starting paths
4. Explain the transformation week by week
5. List included materials
6. Show books or products
7. Add community value
8. Provide testimonials
9. Resolve objections through FAQs
10. Repeat the primary call to action

## 3.5 Soft Repetition

Important messages are repeated without making the page feel pushy.

Repeated elements may include:

- Star rating
- Number of lives changed
- Time required per day
- Instant access
- One-time payment
- Product price
- Main call to action
- Community inclusion

# 4. Visual Direction

## 4.1 Style Classification

Primary style:

> **Editorial Wellness Commerce**

Supporting style labels:

- Nature-led spiritual branding
- Warm minimalist e-commerce
- Premium self-development publishing
- Calm conversion design
- Organic luxury
- Modern heritage wellness

## 4.2 Overall Mood

The visual atmosphere should feel like:

- A quiet morning retreat
- A beautifully printed healing book
- A trusted mentor speaking simply
- Warm sunlight entering a traditional room
- A slow, reassuring journey through nature

The experience should not feel like:

- A tech startup
- A medical clinic
- A loud supplement store
- A generic dropshipping shop
- A dark luxury fashion brand
- A hyper-minimal black-and-white portfolio

# 5. Color System

The following palette is an **approximation derived from the website’s visible imagery and overall art direction**, not an extracted official brand specification.

## 5.1 Primary Palette

| Token | Suggested value | Usage |
|---|---:|---|
| `--color-ivory` | `#F7F3EA` | Main page background |
| `--color-warm-white` | `#FFFDF8` | Cards, product surfaces, elevated sections |
| `--color-parchment` | `#E8DDCE` | Secondary backgrounds, dividers, book-related areas |
| `--color-forest` | `#173B2B` | Primary headings, logo, dark buttons |
| `--color-ink` | `#1D1C19` | Main body text |
| `--color-bark` | `#594432` | Secondary dark accent |
| `--color-moss` | `#6D7046` | Natural accent, icons and subtle highlights |
| `--color-saffron` | `#C47A31` | Robe-inspired accent and selected highlights |
| `--color-golden-sun` | `#D8AF68` | Warm emphasis, badges and atmospheric accents |
| `--color-slate-blue` | `#52687B` | Robe-inspired supporting color |
| `--color-soft-rose` | `#D9A293` | Lotus or emotional-healing accent |
| `--color-muted-line` | `#D8D0C4` | Borders and separators |

## 5.2 Color Ratio

Recommended page distribution:

- 55–65% ivory and warm white
- 15–20% photography
- 10–15% forest and ink typography
- 5–10% parchment and muted neutrals
- Less than 5% saffron, gold, moss, rose or blue accents

## 5.3 Contrast Strategy

Contrast should be clear but never harsh.

Preferred combinations:

- Forest text on ivory
- Ink text on warm white
- White text on forest buttons
- Dark brown text over golden imagery
- Warm white text over dark photography
- Moss accents on parchment

Avoid pure black unless required for accessibility. Prefer dark green, dark brown or softened charcoal.

## 5.4 Background Treatment

Use mostly flat, quiet surfaces.

Recommended:

- Warm ivory page canvas
- White or cream cards
- Parchment feature bands
- Full-bleed photography with soft overlays
- Very subtle natural paper grain when appropriate

Avoid:

- Strong gradients
- Glassmorphism
- Blue-purple glow
- Metallic effects
- High-saturation color blocks
- Heavy patterned backgrounds

# 6. Typography

## 6.1 Typography Character

The typography uses an **editorial serif plus clean sans-serif pairing**.

The serif communicates:

- Wisdom
- Tradition
- Literature
- Calm authority
- Emotional depth

The sans-serif communicates:

- Clarity
- Practicality
- Modern usability
- E-commerce functionality

## 6.2 Suggested Font Pairings

### Option A: Premium and accessible

- Headings: `Cormorant Garamond`
- Body/UI: `Inter`

### Option B: Softer editorial

- Headings: `Lora`
- Body/UI: `Manrope`

### Option C: More classical

- Headings: `Libre Baskerville`
- Body/UI: `DM Sans`

### Option D: Modern book-publishing feel

- Headings: `Source Serif 4`
- Body/UI: `Source Sans 3`

Do not imitate a font from a screenshot without confirming its license and source. Match the emotional qualities rather than forcing an exact copy.

## 6.3 Type Scale

Desktop recommendation:

| Role | Size | Line height | Weight |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Hero headline | 56–72px | 0.98–1.08 | 500–600 |
| Section headline | 40–52px | 1.05–1.15 | 500–600 |
| Card headline | 26–34px | 1.12–1.22 | 500–600 |
| Supporting headline | 20–24px | 1.25 | 600 |
| Body large | 18–20px | 1.55–1.7 | 400 |
| Body | 16–18px | 1.55–1.75 | 400 |
| Small/meta | 13–15px | 1.4–1.55 | 500 |
| Button | 15–17px | 1 | 600 |

Mobile recommendation:

| Role | Size |
|---|---:|
| Hero headline | 40–48px |
| Section headline | 32–38px |
| Card headline | 23–28px |
| Body large | 17–18px |
| Body | 16px |
| Small/meta | 13–14px |

## 6.4 Heading Rules

Headings should:

- Use sentence case
- Remain short and emotionally clear
- Use line breaks intentionally
- Avoid excessive boldness
- Have slightly tight letter spacing
- Carry most of the visual personality

Examples:

- Build a calmer, happier and healthier life.
- Choose your path.
- The 4-week transformation.
- Three books, read at your own pace.
- Begin your healing journey.

## 6.5 Body Copy Rules

Body copy should:

- Use plain, conversational English
- Prefer short paragraphs
- Limit line length to approximately 55–72 characters
- Explain benefits through felt outcomes
- Avoid technical jargon
- Sound reassuring rather than commanding

# 7. Layout System

## 7.1 Page Width

Suggested maximum widths:

| Container | Width |
|---|---:|
| Main content | 1180–1240px |
| Reading content | 680–780px |
| Narrow centered statement | 580–680px |
| Full-bleed visual | 100vw with safe inner padding |

## 7.2 Grid

Desktop:

- 12-column grid
- 24–32px gutters
- 48–80px page margins depending on viewport
- Common splits: 5/7, 6/6 and 4/8

Tablet:

- 8-column grid
- 24px gutters
- 32px margins

Mobile:

- 4-column grid
- 16–20px gutters
- 18–24px page margins

## 7.3 Section Rhythm

The website depends on relaxed vertical pacing.

Recommended spacing:

| Space | Value |
|---|---:|
| Section padding desktop | 96–144px |
| Section padding tablet | 72–96px |
| Section padding mobile | 56–72px |
| Heading to description | 16–24px |
| Description to CTA | 24–36px |
| Card internal padding | 24–36px |
| Grid gap | 24–32px |
| Small stack gap | 8–16px |

Alternate between:

- Wide emotional section
- Compact decision section
- Explanatory content section
- Visual product section
- Social-proof section
- Conversion section

This produces a slow, readable journey.

# 8. Header and Navigation

## 8.1 Header Style

The navigation should be visually quiet and secondary to the content.

Recommended structure:

- Left: Yang Mun wordmark or restrained logo
- Center or left-middle: primary navigation
- Right: region/currency selector, search and cart
- Mobile: logo plus simple menu and cart controls

## 8.2 Navigation Labels

Use simple product-oriented labels:

- Home
- Journey
- Books
- Products
- About

Avoid clever or abstract navigation names.

## 8.3 Header Behavior

- Sticky or semi-sticky behavior is acceptable
- Use a warm solid background
- Add a fine bottom border rather than a shadow
- Keep height between 68–84px
- Use compact sans-serif labels
- Hover treatment should be understated: opacity, underline or subtle color shift

---

# 9. Hero Section

## 9.1 Hero Purpose

The hero establishes emotional relevance before explaining products.

It should contain:

1. A small proof statement
2. A large benefit-led headline
3. A reassuring paragraph
4. Three credibility bullets
5. One primary CTA
6. A compact rating/payment note
7. A large visual or product image

## 9.2 Hero Composition

Recommended desktop layouts:

### Split hero

- Left: text content
- Right: portrait, landscape or product visual
- Ratio: approximately 48/52

### Centered editorial hero

- Centered copy above a wide image
- Narrow text width
- Large atmospheric photograph below

## 9.3 Hero Image Treatment

- Natural, soft light
- Gentle depth of field
- Warm highlights
- Muted shadows
- No harsh studio flash
- Rounded corners, approximately 24–40px
- Avoid small decorative floating objects

## 9.4 Hero CTA

Primary CTA should be:

- Dark forest filled
- White or ivory text
- Rounded but not pill-like
- 48–56px tall
- Horizontally padded 24–32px
- Clear verb-led label

Examples:

- Choose your path
- Start the journey
- Get instant access

# 10. Buttons and Links

## 10.1 Primary Button

```css
.button-primary {
  min-height: 52px;
  padding: 0 28px;
  border-radius: 12px;
  border: 1px solid #173B2B;
  background: #173B2B;
  color: #FFFDF8;
  font-weight: 600;
}
```

Interaction:

- Hover: slightly lighter forest or 2px upward movement
- Active: return to baseline
- Focus: clear accessible outline
- Transition: 160–220ms ease-out

## 10.2 Secondary Button

- Warm-white background
- Forest text
- Thin forest border
- Same dimensions as primary
- No strong shadow

## 10.3 Text Links

- Use underline or arrow
- Keep contrast high
- Use restrained movement on hover
- Avoid bright blue default browser styling

Example:

> Start with the eBook Bundle →

# 11. Cards

## 11.1 Product Path Cards

These cards help visitors choose between two or three routes.

Structure:

- Small badge such as “Most popular”
- Large rounded product image
- Product name
- Current and previous price
- Short testimonial
- Four concise inclusions
- Full-width CTA

Recommended styling:

- Warm-white surface
- 1px muted border
- 20–28px radius
- Minimal or no box shadow
- 28–36px internal padding
- Image radius slightly smaller than card radius

## 11.2 Transformation Cards

Week-based cards should communicate sequence.

Structure:

- Week label
- Editorial heading
- One short explanation
- Clear result statement
- Supporting image

The result line can use stronger weight or a muted highlighted background.

## 11.3 Feature Cards

Feature cards may use:

- One simple emoji or line icon
- Short heading
- Two-line description
- Flat surface
- No unnecessary CTA

Use no more than one visual symbol per card.

## 11.4 Testimonial Cards

- Soft neutral background
- Customer image where available
- Name and rating
- Short first-person quote
- Natural language
- Unequal quote lengths are acceptable
- Avoid highly polished corporate testimonial styling

# 12. Image Language

## 12.1 Subject Matter

Preferred imagery includes:

- The teacher in natural or traditional environments
- People sitting in circles
- Walking paths
- Meditation
- Quiet portraits
- Warm temple interiors
- Forests and water
- Books photographed as meaningful objects
- Hands, journals, tea and simple rituals

## 12.2 Lighting

Use:

- Golden-hour light
- Window light
- Soft overcast light
- Warm interior lamps
- Gentle atmospheric haze
- Controlled highlights
- Moderate contrast

Avoid:

- Cold blue lighting
- Neon highlights
- Harsh flash
- Over-sharpening
- Deep crushed shadows
- Hyper-saturated landscapes

## 12.3 Composition

- Centered or calmly balanced subjects
- Plenty of breathing room
- Strong environmental context
- Slow visual movement
- Natural eye lines
- Human-scale scenes
- Portraits that feel approachable rather than celebrity-like

## 12.4 Image Shape

Common image containers:

- Large vertical product cards
- Wide landscape storytelling images
- Square portraits
- Book-cover proportions
- Rounded rectangles

Suggested radii:

- Small image: 12–16px
- Standard image: 20–28px
- Large hero image: 28–40px

## 12.5 Book-Cover Direction

Book covers combine:

- Large classical serif title
- Restrained subtitle
- Central portrait or spiritual motif
- Warm cream, brown, gold or saffron palette
- Minimal graphic clutter
- Symmetrical composition
- Soft painterly or photographic finish

---

# 13. Iconography

The interface uses extremely simple visual symbols.

Recommended:

- Fine line icons
- Small nature-inspired symbols
- Minimal emojis where they improve comprehension
- Checkmarks for inclusions
- Stars for ratings
- Arrows for directional links

Icon rules:

- Stroke width: 1.5–2px
- Size: 18–24px
- Color: forest, bark or moss
- Use one icon family consistently
- Do not mix filled 3D icons with line icons

Avoid:

- Glossy icons
- Colorful SaaS icon sets
- Large floating icon decorations
- Excessive badges
- Cartoon illustrations

# 14. Borders, Radius and Elevation

## 14.1 Radius Scale

```css
:root {
  --radius-small: 8px;
  --radius-medium: 12px;
  --radius-card: 22px;
  --radius-image: 28px;
  --radius-hero: 36px;
  --radius-pill: 999px;
}
```

Use pill radius only for compact labels, not every button.

## 14.2 Borders

- 1px solid muted neutral
- Low contrast
- Often preferable to shadows
- Dividers should feel like fine printed rules

Suggested border:

```css
border: 1px solid #D8D0C4;
```

## 14.3 Shadows

Use very sparingly.

Suggested card shadow:

```css
box-shadow: 0 12px 36px rgba(51, 40, 28, 0.06);
```

Most content cards should work with no shadow.

# 15. Trust and Conversion Elements

## 15.1 Social Proof

Trust indicators should appear near key decisions.

Examples:

- 4.9/5 rating
- Lives changed
- Books sold
- Instant delivery
- One-time payment
- Customer quote
- Community included

## 15.2 Price Presentation

Pricing uses strong clarity:

- Current price shown first
- Previous price struck through
- Savings shown nearby
- No complicated pricing tables
- One clear purchase action

Suggested hierarchy:

```text
$49.99  $99.99  Save 50%
```

The discount should not use aggressive red. Use forest, bark, moss or saffron.

## 15.3 Choice Architecture

Limit primary product paths to two or three.

For example:

- The guided journey
- The eBook bundle
- The complete collection

One option may carry a “Most popular” or “Best value” badge.

## 15.4 FAQ

Use a simple accordion.

Design:

- Full-width rows
- Fine separators
- Large readable question
- Small plus/minus icon
- Comfortable vertical padding
- No boxed accordion card for every item unless necessary

# 16. Content Voice

## 16.1 Tone

The voice is:

- Gentle
- Direct
- Reassuring
- Simple
- Emotionally aware
- Non-technical
- Hopeful
- Practical

## 16.2 Message Pattern

Effective copy often follows this sequence:

> You are not broken.  
> This feeling has an understandable cause.  
> A small daily practice can help.  
> Here is the path.  
> You only need a few minutes.  
> You will not have to do it alone.

## 16.3 Benefit Language

Prefer felt outcomes:

- Sleep more deeply
- Feel lighter
- Quiet the mind
- Restore energy
- Release emotional weight
- Reconnect with yourself
- Build a calmer daily rhythm

Avoid vague corporate phrases:

- Unlock your potential
- Leverage a holistic framework
- Optimize personal performance
- Revolutionary transformation ecosystem

## 16.4 CTA Language

Good:

- Start the journey
- Choose your path
- Get the bundle
- Begin your healing journey
- Get instant access

Avoid:

- Buy now!!!
- Hurry
- Don’t miss out
- Act fast
- Transform instantly

# 17. Motion and Interaction

The visual language supports subtle, almost invisible motion.

## 17.1 Recommended Motion

- Fade and rise on section entry
- Gentle image scale on hover
- Smooth accordion opening
- Soft button lift
- Slow underline reveal
- Simple cart drawer transition
- Optional parallax at very low intensity

## 17.2 Timing

| Interaction | Duration |
|---|---:|
| Button hover | 160–220ms |
| Card hover | 200–280ms |
| Accordion | 240–320ms |
| Section reveal | 500–750ms |
| Page transition | 300–450ms |

## 17.3 Motion Rules

- Use ease-out for entry
- Use ease-in-out for state changes
- Translate elements by only 4–12px
- Scale images no more than 1.02–1.04
- Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`

Avoid:

- Bouncy spring animations
- Rotating text
- Marquee overload
- Fast parallax
- Cursor-following effects
- Animated gradients
- Constantly moving decorative elements

---

# 18. Responsive Behavior

## 18.1 Mobile Priorities

On mobile:

1. Preserve emotional headline impact
2. Keep the primary CTA visible early
3. Stack product choices vertically
4. Retain large readable imagery
5. Shorten long supporting text
6. Use accordions for detail
7. Keep tap targets at least 44px
8. Maintain generous side padding

## 18.2 Mobile Layout Rules

- Single-column by default
- Images may appear before text where emotional context matters
- Center-aligned hero content is acceptable
- Left-align longer reading sections
- Product buttons should become full width
- Avoid sticky bottom bars unless they remain visually quiet
- Keep body text at least 16px

## 18.3 Mobile Spacing

- Page padding: 18–24px
- Section spacing: 56–72px
- Card gap: 20–24px
- Card padding: 22–28px
- Button height: 50–56px

---

# 19. Accessibility

The calm aesthetic must not reduce usability.

Requirements:

- Minimum WCAG AA contrast for text
- 16px minimum body text
- Visible keyboard focus states
- Descriptive image alt text
- Semantic heading order
- Large tap targets
- Text labels for important icons
- Captions for video teachings
- Reduced-motion support
- Do not communicate status through color alone
- Ensure struck-through prices remain understandable to screen readers

# 22. Do and Don’t

## Do

- Use warm, natural neutrals
- Let photography carry emotion
- Use serif typography for major storytelling
- Present only a few clear product routes
- Repeat proof near conversion points
- Give sections generous breathing room
- Use concrete outcomes
- Keep interactions gentle
- Use rounded images and cards
- Maintain a warm human tone

## Don’t

- Use bright SaaS gradients
- Add decorative UI without purpose
- Crowd the hero
- Use more than two dominant CTAs in one viewport
- Add heavy shadows
- Use sharp black borders
- Overuse pills and badges
- Make spiritual claims visually sensational
- Use generic corporate stock photography
- Turn every section into a boxed card
- Use aggressive urgency patterns

# 24. Reusable Creative Prompt

Use the following prompt when generating related website imagery:

> Create a warm editorial wellness photograph inspired by an ancient wisdom retreat. Show a calm, approachable teacher in a natural or traditional setting with soft golden-hour light, muted forest greens, warm ivory, weathered wood, saffron and slate-blue fabric. The composition should feel peaceful, premium, human and timeless, with gentle depth of field, subtle atmospheric haze and ample negative space. Avoid neon colors, futuristic elements, glossy commercial lighting, exaggerated mystical effects and busy backgrounds.

---

# 25. Final Design Summary

The Yang Mun design language can be reduced to six rules:

1. **Use warmth instead of brightness.**
2. **Use editorial hierarchy instead of decorative complexity.**
3. **Use nature and human presence as the main visual language.**
4. **Use structured journeys to make transformation understandable.**
5. **Use repeated trust signals without aggressive selling.**
6. **Make every screen feel calm, readable and emotionally safe.**

The strongest visual formula is:

> **Warm ivory canvas + forest typography + editorial serif headings + golden natural photography + rounded product imagery + restrained conversion components.**
